# Notifications and announcements

## Overview

Notifications presents current notifications and their history in one immediately available notification center: a quiet header row, two quick-settings tiles and one card per notification. Producer and summary form a strong first line; lifecycle detail, body and available actions follow in source order. Actions remain local to their notification and do not imply that historical targets still accept commands.

## Anatomy

The renderer emits `.surface-popup[data-mode="notifications"]`, a heading, a `p[role="status"][aria-live="off"]` and one `.surface-controls[role="group"]`. The controls begin with Close, Refresh, Do not disturb and critical interruption. Each entry is a flat sequence: `p.notification-text[data-notification-content$=":summary"]`, optional `p.notification-text[data-notification-content$=":body"]`, then zero or more `button[data-surface-control^="notification:"]`. The summary label is the producer application and summary joined by a colon; its detail is `Live` or `History ·` followed by the source lifecycle. The body is optional. There is no entry container: each card is drawn from its consecutive pieces.

The controls are one wrapping row flow with 8px between neighbours on a row and no gap between rows, so card pieces meet without seams. Close and Refresh share the first row as quiet controls at least 2.5rem high; Refresh takes the remaining width and keeps its detail under its label. Do not disturb and critical interruption follow 10px below as two quick-settings tiles side by side, each at least 6.25rem high, with 14px top and side and 12px bottom padding, 12px corners, a 1px hairline (text at 9%), the text-at-5% tile fill and a 1px top highlight; a 20px glyph sits 12px above the name (14px at 600), followed by its On or Off state (12px secondary).

Each notification is one card. The first card starts 16px below the tiles and later cards follow 8px apart. The summary opens the card with 12px upper corners, 12px top and 14px inline padding: producer and summary at 14/20 semibold, with the lifecycle detail at 12/16 at the inline end of the same line, wrapping beneath when space runs out. The body follows 4px below at 14/20 regular. Text followed by actions keeps 12px beneath it; text that ends the card closes it with 14px of padding and 12px lower corners. Actions form one row that never wraps, inset 14px from the card edges: producer actions are pills with fully rounded ends, at least 36px high, with centred 13px labels at 600, text at 9% over the card fill and a text-at-14% edge, and they share the row; Dismiss notification is a quiet trailing control in secondary text with a 16px close glyph. The first action paints the card band behind the whole row, card edge to card edge, through 12px below the actions, with the card's lower corners. The popup uses the shell skin's 20px inset and the 6px focus gutter; its renderer fills the host allocation, with a preferred documentation width of 480px. Long text wraps without clamping; the controls scroll with focus paint visible.

## States and behaviour

Live entries expose only their current producer-provided actions and Dismiss notification. Expired, invoked, dismissed, closed, disconnected, unknown and unavailable entries retain their factual history. A focused action whose target expires remains in the keyed projection with `data-focus-only="true"`, `aria-disabled="true"` and unavailable wording; it remains keyboard focusable and cannot activate. Disabled styling retains opacity 1. Refresh reads current targets and never repeats an action. During a read it says `Reading current targets…`; a service failure gives its source reason and the adapter announcement directs the user to refresh. An unknown action result says it is not confirmed and will not be repeated.

`notifications:dnd` and `notifications:critical-interrupt` are independent buttons with their actual `aria-pressed` values. Do not disturb preserves history and suppresses notification speech. Critical interruption is consent for critical arrivals only when Do not disturb is off. Their state wording is visible; CSS does not change the semantics or infer state from copy. Refresh and Close remain quiet controls. A pressed tile takes the brand tint (the action colour at 28% in Night, 12% in Dawn), a brand-text edge at 50% and a brand-text glyph, and its On wording turns primary text; the On wording is the non-colour cue. Producer actions are neutral pills, and Dismiss notification is a quiet trailing control; neither uses a brand fill. A card whose summary or body is directly followed by an action that is not focus-only presents as live: the live card fill (text at 6% over the raised surface in Night, the raised surface in Dawn), a text-at-9% edge, primary text, a 1px top highlight, a soft shadow under its band, and its lifecycle detail as an uppercase Live badge of 11px brand text at 600 with 0.06em tracking on the action colour at 28%. Every other card presents as history: the history fill (text at 2.5% over the surface), a text-at-6% edge, secondary text and the lifecycle detail as plain secondary text. An expired focused action keeps its pill edge on the recessed fill with secondary text and the shared unavailable cue, so it stays visibly unavailable, and its band takes the history fill without shadow. High contrast draws an unavailable producer action with a dashed unfocused-edge border. Neither a clear-all operation nor categories, timestamps or toast presentation is introduced.

Open and close are immediate. Incoming entries, changing status and action availability never animate, flash or invoke a portal. Pointer hover changes only the enabled fill (a pill to text at 15%, Dismiss to the 7% hover with primary text, a pressed tile to the action colour at 36%), and press deepens a pill to text at 20%; keyboard focus uses the shared opaque gap and 2px blue outline, including focus-only unavailable actions, and a focused pill stays above its band. The existing controller maintains publication, lease, focus and action authority. No styling makes a held press act on a replacement target.

## Keyboard and accessibility

Tab and Shift+Tab traverse rendered controls; Enter or Space activates an enabled button. Escape and Close follow the host's existing dismissal and focus-return policy. Scroll padding and scroll margin retain 6px of focus paint. At 200% text and 320px effective width, labels, details and actions wrap and remain reachable. Forced colours use shared system-colour focus and boundaries: an opaque Canvas panel, Canvas text pieces and bands with CanvasText borders, ButtonFace and ButtonText controls with ButtonText pill borders, GrayText unavailable actions, and pressed tiles in SelectedItem with SelectedItemText. Reduced motion, reduced transparency, high contrast and effects-off preserve content and sharp focus. Status paragraphs stay `aria-live="off"`.

`OutcomeAnnouncements` owns admission and correlation; `Announcement.view` renders the single elected live owner with polite or consent-gated assertive delivery. No second live region is added by this component or its static specimen. Focus changes and rerenders do not generate new announcements. Multiple arrivals are summarized by the existing owner rather than spoken separately.

## Shared outcome guidance for D01

Use only typed protocol outcomes. Pending uses neutral waiting wording and a static clock when a typed presentation hook supplies it. Submitted states that the action was sent, not completed. Refused uses a neutral surface, danger octagon and explicit reason when typed as refused. Unknown uses a warning question diamond and unconfirmed wording; Refresh only reads. Positive checks require confirmed completion. Untyped notification service reasons remain neutral and are never classified from text. These glyphs require typed renderer projections; this CSS does not create them or duplicate announcements.

## Evidence and renderer requirements

Source authority is commit `bc3daf7b80ac7295a1ba1e56639a0e130e520a3c`, especially `Surface.elm`, `SurfaceRenderer.elm`, `Notifications.elm`, `Desktop.elm`, `OutcomeAnnouncements.elm`, `Announcement.elm` and `AdapterNotice.elm`. The native history captures show low hierarchy between summary, body and lifecycle and large outlined actions. The card treatment separates them without inventing a wrapper. The window-feedback capture establishes unconfirmed language and read-only refresh.

Material list and button captures inform grouping and target sizing; GNOME notification guidance informs concise action placement; Fluent and Windows notification captures inform actionable message hierarchy; KDE layout and Apple HIG captures inform alignment and restraint; WCAG focus captures inform unobscured focus. Warlock's Lacquer material and exact tokens govern where platform patterns differ.

The shared foundation supplies fonts, control states, focus ring and scrollport layout. D01 integrates the area stylesheet after those rules. The pressed policy tile uses its existing On wording as the non-colour cue, with the brand tint, edge and glyph. Disabled and focus-only controls retain their full focus treatment. A typed outcome glyph requires an explicit renderer projection before implementation. The existing summary combines producer and summary into one text node, so CSS does not style them independently. Cards survive arbitrary action counts because the first action paints the band and the action row never wraps. Card edges come from adjacency: a summary opens a card, text followed by a summary or ending the list closes it, and the last action before the next card takes the closing inset. Dismiss is recognised by its fixed `:dismiss:` identity suffix. No CSS selector depends on dynamic identity values, text parsing or a fabricated wrapper.

Live and history are inferred from that structure, not from the notification's lifecycle: a live notification whose first action is focus-only presents as history. A per-notification wrapper that contains the summary, body and actions of one notification, carrying a typed lifecycle attribute (live or history, with the source lifecycle), is required so the card boundary, the Live badge and the history treatment follow the actual lifecycle. The wrapper must keep every keyed piece, identity, accessible name and source order, and add no live region.

## Expiry sequence and specimen

[Open Notifications in the desktop](../../test-drive/index.html?scenario=notifications). The [scenario fixture](scenario.json) specifies the live-to-expired sequence at a stable service and incarnation. The renderer keeps the keyed control when its target expires and gives it `data-focus-only="true"` and `aria-disabled="true"`; its message is absent. The controller retains authority over activation. A real host test records focus before and after the observation, attempts Enter and Space, and checks both the emitted effects and the elected announcement owner. The [expiry exercise](expiry.html) supplies the live and expired service observations to the compiled controller and renderer. The static gallery represents the final state only.

The bounded Night runtime fixture uses a 420px host and a dedicated controls scrollport. The Dawn comparison shows the same live and historical content at its natural height. Content-sized comparisons show empty history, unavailable service with refresh pending, unknown and refused results. The examples include reduced motion, effects off and high contrast. The text scale control changes the document root once, including rem-based text and targets. Long text and multiple live actions exercise wrapping and source order. The specimen loads this stylesheet without the shell skin, so its cards sit on an opaque surface and its glyph slots are empty.
