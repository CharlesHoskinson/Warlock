`release-inventory.qnt` models two file identities, three component identities, payload, a set-valued ownership relation, containment edges, shipped identities, scan error and review snapshots/decision. Independent additions/removals, edge and scan mutations and reviews can interleave. Review snapshots represent an ideal coherent observation; acceptedSnapshotCovered independently states exactly one owner per payload and no extra mapping, acceptedSnapshotReachable separately states a root or path of length at most two for each shipped component, and acceptedSnapshotComplete excludes a scan error. Three components suffice for two-edge paths. Mutation after review preserves its historical decision and marks it changed; it is explicitly not a statement that current files remain covered. Scan error abstracts a reported incomplete walk, not silent omissions or adversarial mutation *during* the actual scan. Owner addition makes its component shipped and edge addition makes both endpoints shipped: build-only intermediary nodes and other catalog validation are omitted. Hashes, symlinks, actual byte identity, notice/disposition details, categories, provenance, legal authority, operating-system behavior and builder/archive interleavings are outside the model. The review's snapshot guard and assertions express the same requirement using different formulations; they check the abstraction, not the production algorithm.

Disconnected self and mutual cycles are refused because they lack a file-owning shipped root. A rooted cycle is allowed by the actual reachability algorithm. The test suite explicitly checks both; no blanket acyclicity requirement is inferred. Negative tests include unaccounted payload, duplicate mappings, disconnected cycles, scan errors, and stale accepted snapshots; positive tests include acceptance, rooted cycles and restoration. The Python cases exercise actual `review` with isolated temporary fixtures, including a mocked walk error; they do not demonstrate filesystem races or a real package.
