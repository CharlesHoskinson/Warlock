Build verifies and copies, reviews and reinventories, verifies again, constructs manifest/archive, audits, then hashes and reports. These are multiple filesystem operations.

The single state record has four independent abstract facts: coverage, evidence (provenance/notices/review), locked inputs, and filesystem/archive identity. Separate actions establish each; review, package and audit capture revisions only when their relevant facts and prior receipts permit them. Mutation independently advances a bounded revision and invalidates all facts; report may follow a historical audit even after mutation. `safeReceipt` asserts matching nonnegative historical receipts whenever report succeeds. It is not an assertion that current bytes remain valid. `currentOutcome` deliberately asserts that stronger freshness property and has a retained counterexample. The revision is an opaque identity class, not a cryptographic model. `cover` and the other fact-establishing actions abstract detailed peer predicates, so this model cannot prove those facts correspond to actual inputs; they can also be established without actual verification. No peer predicates were imported while peer models were still being developed.

Peer sketch review against the original oracle and source boundaries (summaries received via AgentBus, not a review of frozen models): inventory lane covers file ownership, shipped reachability and independent mutation; verify that build-only components and every category/notice are covered by the evidence lane, and don't equate rooted reachability with independently complete provenance. Provenance lane captures actual versus pinned identities and review snapshots; legal authority and source-to-binary linkage remain outside scope. Packaging lane describes separated lock, verify, stage, review, manifest, tar and audit phases; preserve interphase mutation and don't promote a snapshot to a current guarantee. Transport lane models member identity and aliases, but reports that its atomic audit includes containment not supplied by `audit_archive` alone; the connection to `_inventory` and intervening writes must be explicit. These are review cautions, not completed C1–C6/R1–R2 runtime audits of peer models; their files and results were not frozen when this note was written.

For this model: grouped record and typed phase; every action assigns the sole variable, and `step` includes all nine actions. Every major action has an explicit positive test (`facts`, `happy`, `changedAfterReview`/`changedAfterPackage`/`changedAfterAudit`); four negative tests block missing facts. Logic is deliberately compact in guarded actions rather than factored into pure predicates. `safeReceipt` holds initially and no counterexample was found in the sampled run; this is bounded evidence. The guard and receipts share conditions, so it checks the chosen historical discipline rather than independently proving production implements it. Deadlocks on invalidation before audit are intentional abstraction of refusal; recovery/retry is omitted.

The named `changedAfterAudit` witness reaches a stale reported state. Independently, sampled `currentOutcome` produced a counterexample (seed `0x1bf63b38ace754`), with audit revision 0 and reported revision 2. This is a model counterexample to freshness, not proof of a production release defect. The Python check directly calls `audit_archive`: matching payload/archive passes, changed payload fails on re-audit, and a write after the first audit is possible. It does not exercise an interleaving inside `build` or show that an actual release consumer receives changed bytes. Assess immutable staging/publication or end-to-end identity checking if a current-byte guarantee is required; do not weaken the original oracle.
